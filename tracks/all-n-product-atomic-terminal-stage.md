1. a historical back edge gives PX294;
2. a cyclic single layer gives PX311;
3. opposite historical orientations give the two-history transposition PX315;
4. any cycle in the full historical union gives PX330;

   \[
   \boxed{s\le\Delta_0+1.}

## 2. Atomic strict-sign hierarchy

For one admissible transposition `e`, let `d_e` be assigned old destruction and
let

\[
c_e=\mu(f_e^1)+\mu(f_e^2)+\lambda_e
\]

be its complete new-triple count.  Then

\[
\boxed{\Delta\Phi\le-d_e+c_e.}
\]

If `d_e>c_e`, the switch strictly improves.  Otherwise one of the three
creation terms is at least `ceil(d_e/3)`, giving either

\[
\boxed{\text{clean-star order }\ge
\left\lceil\frac{d_e}{3K}\right\rceil}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{\text{loaded-line occupancy }\ge
\left\lceil\frac{d_e}{3}\right\rceil+2.}
\]

Every endpoint has at least `m-1-2Delta` admissible partners, so this applies
throughout the nonterminal range `m>=2Delta+2`.

For packet defects the same statement uses `d_e=w_e`.  Blocked packet
corrections are already selected-star children, so the linear packet residue
