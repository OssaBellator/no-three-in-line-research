## Completeness and minimum-anchor mode

The complete-entering-batch anchor deletion is a rigorous aggressive subbranch,
but its forcing conclusions are branch-local. Complete search uses viable-child
unions or the rank-three prescription split.

The rank-three split is disjointly refinable and has depth at most `2n^2`.
Terminal leaves at one target-resolution stage compress to at most

\[
8\binom{n^2}{3}
\]

fixed labelled-triple set-family classes. Differently masked unions are not
silently treated as one host.

For an actual minimum state `S`, restrictions preserving `S` preserve one
minimum. A selected target and labelled anchor prescription can be forced using
at most

\[
2n^2-2n
\]

outside-anchor deletions. Condition the full current cylinder before contraction;
then the residual minimum face remains a genuine matching-host or joint-state
minimum for the induced objective.

## Product descent and cross-factor potential

With a fixed compatible core and exact product, every real triple is constant,
pure in one factor, or a low-rank coupling atom with an exact Cartesian
occurrence box. Coupling atoms delete minimum-preservingly or contract on the
minimum face.

Selecting the skeleton class and coordinate fibre containing one actual minimum
preserves that minimum. Large protected cores therefore enter exact
protected/free products, coupling normalization, essential-core contraction and
strict prefix descent; they are not terminal obstructions.

## Target handoff and corrected signatures

A same-value minimum that destroys a physical target permits deletion of both
layer copies of one omitted target cell. This preserves a minimum and removes the
target until genuine restoration.

If every target-destroying bank state lies above the minimum by integer gap `g`,
destroying load `D` creates at least `D+g` new triples. Every new triple has an
entering physical cell.

A recurrent basic signature `(e,U)` does **not** fix the other two layer labels
after conditioning only on `e`. The exact corrected partition has at most four
augmented classes `(e,U,P)`. Rank-two transfer is host-representable only inside
one fixed assignment class.

## Robust-surplus and simultaneous execution

New triples split by physical entry rank.

- Rank one gives an entering-cell line decomposition: a loaded old target line or
  a cell-disjoint simultaneous secant star.
- Rank at least two gives one entering pair with many third cells on one nonaxis
  line and a majority-layer heavy-line profile.

Simultaneity removes the generic historical-star losses:

- common-layer outside cells already form one partial matching and absorb
  directly;
- in a cross-layer star, each endpoint side is a partial matching, and one whole
  side absorbs while the other permutation remains fixed; the rematching board
  has forbidden degree two;
- loaded old lines and entering-pair lines absorb all majority-layer cells outside
  the protected core.

No-growth outputs are large protected cores and therefore enter exact product
descent. Loaded old profiles have explicit target destruction bounds.

## Static target-hypergraph packing

The physical targets of a dirty minimum form a 3-uniform hypergraph. For every
`q>=2`, either there are `q` vertex-disjoint targets or a selected-cell cover of
size at most `3(q-1)`. In the cover branch, one selected cell lies in at least

\[
\left\lceil\frac{\Phi(S)}{3(q-1)}\right\rceil
\]

targets, which again yields a loaded line or simultaneous star.

A disjoint target bank has a simultaneous escape: at least `ceil(q/2)` targets
share one majority layer. Rematching that layer while forbidding its old
permutation and the fixed opposite permutation destroys all those targets in one
degree-two Hall bank state. This yields improvement, same-value target handoff,
or a robust-surplus episode of proportional load.

## Branch-wide protected capacity

Protected growth cannot restart indefinitely after owner changes. Fix routing
recurrence threshold `lambda>=2` and define

\[
\mathfrak P(N,h,\lambda)
=
(h+1)(2N+1)
\sum_{m=1}^{N}
2m(2m^2+m+1)
\left(
1+\left\lfloor\frac{(\lambda-1)m^2}{2}\right\rfloor
\right).
\]

Unless one routing-support edge recurs `lambda` times at one owner, the total
owner-labelled protected growth over the complete closure branch is at most
`mathfrak P(N,h,lambda)`. Hence positive-growth episodes are globally finite,
not merely finite at one selector owner.

After this capacity is exhausted, every further target execution must produce
large-core product descent, loaded-line or target-cell recurrence, lost-minimum
ancestry, recurrent routing support, envelope expansion, finite base handling,
or strict potential improvement.

## Corrections retained

- Sequential two-layer rematching may reoccupy an old first-layer cell.
- Historical target lines and stars are not simultaneous families.
- One edge return may serve several neutralisations in one absence run.
- Removing one essential edge gives Hall deficiency exactly one.
- Aggressive batch deletion is branch-local.
- Differently masked leaf unions are not automatically one matching host.
- An empty intersection host has no assigned minimum.
- Conditioning on one support edge does not fix a residual pair.
- A one-layer line-clean cylinder does not by itself remove an opposite-layer
  selected cell.
- Direct whole-subbank absorption is used only for arms simultaneously present in
  one permutation state; historical stars still require the generic extraction.

## Bottom line

There is no complete proof. Through **CMR1093**, local completeness,
same-vertex-set dynamics, structural product descent, target-hypergraph packing,
robust-surplus geometry, simultaneous star/line execution and owner-reset
protected capacity all have exact finite normal forms. The remaining prime-power
problem is the residual structural loss/restoration loop, not uncontrolled local
target geometry or protected-growth resets.
