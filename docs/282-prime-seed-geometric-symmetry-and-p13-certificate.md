# Geometric seed symmetries and a verified prime-thirteen certificate

The relative-permutation formulation separates algebra from geometry, but it
also creates a possible false simplification: arbitrary row or column
relabeling is not a Euclidean symmetry of the board.  One may quotient by the
eight geometric symmetries of the square and swap the two layers, but one may
not normalize `sigma` to the identity or replace an arbitrary relative
permutation by a conjugate using an unrestricted coordinate permutation.

This chapter records the exact symmetry group available to finite search and
adds a verified seed for `p=13`, extending the stored certificate range from
`p<=11` to `p<=13` along the prime-minus-one sequence.

## 1. Geometric square symmetries

Let `J(x)=n+1-x`.  The eight board maps are generated by

```text
(x,y) -> (J(x),y),
(x,y) -> (x,J(y)),
(x,y) -> (y,x).
```

They are the rotations and reflections of the square.

### Proposition PP3bdl -- PROVED

Every square symmetry maps a saturated no-three seed to another saturated
no-three seed.  Swapping the two permutation layers also preserves the seed.
Under all sixteen operations obtained from square symmetry and layer swap, the
relative cycle partition is unchanged.

#### Proof

A square symmetry is an invertible affine isometry of the Euclidean plane.  It
maps rows and columns to rows or columns, preserves distinctness and
collinearity, and therefore preserves saturation and the no-three property.
The image of one permutation graph is again a permutation graph, possibly
after transposition.

Horizontal or vertical reflection conjugates the relative permutation by the
corresponding coordinate reversal or leaves it unchanged.  Transposition
replaces it by a conjugate of its inverse, as in PP3bcx.  Layer swap replaces
it by its inverse.  Conjugacy and inversion preserve cycle lengths. ∎

These operations are valid exact symmetry breaking for a solver.

## 2. Arbitrary coordinate relabeling is not a symmetry

### Proposition PP3bdm -- PROVED

An arbitrary permutation of the row labels or column labels does not preserve
Euclidean collinearity.  Consequently one cannot, without loss of generality,
normalize

```text
sigma=id
```

or quotient fixed-relative searches by arbitrary conjugacy classes of `pi`.
Only conjugacies induced by geometric square symmetries are automatically
valid.

#### Proof

On `[4]^2`, the cells

```text
(1,1),(2,2),(3,3)
```

are collinear.  Relabel rows by the permutation fixing `1,2` and swapping
`3,4`.  Their images

```text
(1,1),(2,2),(3,4)
```

are not collinear.  Applying the inverse relabeling gives an example in which
a noncollinear triple becomes collinear.

Algebraically applying `sigma^(-1)` to every row would turn the first layer
into the identity graph, but that graph contains `n` collinear diagonal points
for `n>=3`.  The relabeling is therefore not a Euclidean symmetry and cannot be
used as a normalization in the seed CSP. ∎

This explains why two relative permutations of the same abstract cycle type
may have very different geometric feasibility.

## 3. A new exact seed at p=13

Put `n=12` and define

```text
sigma=
[7,8,12,3,4,1,2,11,5,6,9,10],

tau=
[5,4,7,1,8,11,12,9,2,10,3,6].
```

### Theorem PP3bdn -- VERIFIED FINITELY

The union of the two permutation graphs above is a saturated no-three-in-line
set of size `24` on `[12]^2`.  It is therefore a valid prime-minus-one seed for

```text
p=13.
```

#### Verification

Both arrays are permutations of `[12]` and differ in every column.  Hence the
union has exactly two points in every row and column.  The exact checker tests
all

```text
binom(24,3)=2024
```

integer determinants and finds none equal to zero. ∎

The certificate is now included in

```text
experiments/prime-minus-one-seed-examples.json.
```

## 4. Relative structure of the p=13 seed

For the preceding certificate,

```text
pi=sigma^(-1) o tau
  =[9,5,1,6,2,8,3,11,7,12,4,10].
```

### Proposition PP3bdo -- VERIFIED FINITELY

The relative cycle partition is

```text
[4,4,2,2].
```

Equivalently, the saturated incidence graph has alternating component lengths

```text
[8,8,4,4].
```

The board `[12]^2` has `898` maximal grid lines containing at least three
cells, of which `874` are nonaxis.  The certificate has occupancy at most two
on every one of them.

#### Verification

The relative-cycle checker reconstructs `pi`, follows all cycles, enumerates
maximal lines from primitive directions, and evaluates their selected
occupancies exactly. ∎

Thus connected relative `n`-cycles are not the only useful finite search
class: the first new certificate beyond `p=11` has four relative components.

## 5. Exact finite-search quotient

### Corollary PP3bdp -- PROVED

An exact finite search may safely quotient candidate pairs only by:

1. the eight geometric symmetries of the square;
2. layer swap; and
3. duplicate encodings that leave the selected cell set unchanged.

Relative cycle partition is a valid invariant for splitting the search, but
abstract conjugacy of derangements is not a valid geometric equivalence.

#### Proof

Use PP3bdl for the valid group and PP3bdm for the failure of unrestricted
coordinate relabeling.  The cycle partition is invariant under the valid group
by PP3bdl, but equal cycle partition alone supplies no board isometry. ∎

### Corollary PP3bdq -- PROVED

The certified prime-minus-one range in the stored regression suite is now

```text
p=3,5,7,11,13.
```

The observed relative cycle partitions are

```text
p=3:  [2]
p=5:  [2,2]
p=7:  [6]
p=11: [10]
p=13: [4,4,2,2].
```

These finite certificates show that both connected and disconnected relative
cycle types occur.  They do not imply an asymptotic family.  The remaining
global theorem is still feasibility for every sufficiently large prime.
