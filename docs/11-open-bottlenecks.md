# Open bottlenecks and research roadmap

## 1. Why inherited context remains necessary

Exact normalized four-endpoint traps show that current-board type and raw triple
count alone cannot force global descent.  The prime-power and prime-field routes
still require inherited parent load, protected reserve, deletion ancestry, token
return, or closure-envelope expansion.

The live branch now preserves those labels through line-clean selectors,
protected matchings, sparse product skeletons, essential contractions, strict
child routing, mixed-child deletion, and forced-certificate ancestry.  No line,
token, edge, skeleton, factor, or certificate charge is anonymous.

## 3. Current principal bottleneck: descending-path budget aggregation

A selected prime-power factor path now has a monotone structural execution.
At each factor-envelope owner:

- routing changes pay at least two entering and two leaving physical edges;
- a fixed routing skeleton gives an exact product over strict child factors;
- active cross-child atoms are deleted through nonessential edges;
- after at most `d^2` such deletions, the product is clean, reduces to one strict
  child factor, or exposes a forced essential certificate;
- strict child recursion lowers envelope side by at least a factor of `p` and
  lowers factor side;
- forced certificates have finite owner stock and can escape only through
  deletion, routing churn, or genuinely entering factor edges.

The unresolved theorem is no longer local matching structure.  It is to combine
these finite and decreasing structural moves with the inherited global
potential.

### Required aggregation theorem

Along one owner-labelled descending path, prove that at least one of the
following occurs before the finite structural budgets are exhausted:

1. a globally candidate-conflict-free state is selected;
2. inherited target load strictly decreases;
3. protected reserve is consumed by a quantitatively positive amount;
4. permanent deletion ancestry increases;
5. a full-token return or entering-edge recreation charge is paid;
6. the closure envelope strictly expands to one of its finitely many ancestors.

A useful potential should be lexicographic or weighted across:

\[
(
\text{closure-envelope depth},
\text{factor-envelope side},
\text{factor side},
\text{protected reserve},
\text{host-edge stock},
\text{target load}
).
\]

Static token consumption alone cannot be one coordinate because exact
restoring cycles exist.

## 4. Remaining technical bottlenecks

### Target-load splice for deletions and contractions

CMR334 supplies the correct rank-zero/rank-one average in the line-clean bank,
and CMR664--CMR683 now supply finite deletion and strict child recursion.  The
next lemma must show that the deletion/contraction path cannot preserve or
increase inherited target load indefinitely without paying one of the existing
wall, prefix, carry, token, or envelope certificates.

### Recurrent entering-edge aggregation

Routing change and forced-certificate escape both produce owner-labelled
entering edges.  Existing theorems price each occurrence by full-token incidence
and recreation support.  What remains is a single branch-level inequality which
prevents the same physical edge from being reused across nested factor owners
without reintroduction, deletion ancestry, or envelope expansion.

### Forced-core terminal geometry

A forced cross-child triple is present in every state of its product owner.  Its
escape is paid, but a complete proof still needs to convert long persistence of
such a triple into target-load descent, protected-reserve depletion, or a larger
inherited parent move.

### Low-height and prime-field transfer

The remaining thin quotient/carry signatures must be integrated into the same
budget, and the owner-labelled endpoint must be rebuilt for prime fields outside
the balanced recursive families.

### Arbitrary side lengths

Even a complete prime-power theorem would not cover every `n`.  Remaining
options include additional balanced prime factorizations, controlled products,
CRT absorption, or patching between nearby admissible sizes.

## 5. Open lemmas in recommended order

1. **Descending-path potential.** Define a branch potential compatible with
   mixed deletion, essential contraction, strict child descent, and envelope
   expansion.
2. **Entering-edge owner aggregation.** Deduplicate routing and certificate-
   escape entering edges across nested factor owners and attach reintroduction
   or permanent-deletion payment.
3. **Target-load change under child deletion.** Bound the inherited target-load
   change caused by one matching-preserving child-edge deletion and one complete-
   core contraction.
4. **Forced-certificate persistence.** Convert a long fixed forced triple into a
   parent-bank improvement, reserve loss, token return, or envelope expansion.
5. **Low-height and prime-field transfer.** Integrate thin carry signatures and
   rebuild the endpoint for prime fields.
6. **Balanced-family and CRT assembly.** Cover arbitrary side lengths.

## 6. Computational priorities

- Enumerate complete structural paths for small prime-power parents and record
  target load, reserve, deleted edges, routing churn, and envelope depth at every
  step.
- Search for small integer weights making the proposed lexicographic coordinates
  monotone on all verified path transitions.
- Measure how often one physical entering edge can recur under nested factor
  owners before reintroduction or envelope expansion.
- Test forced cross-child triples against the existing joint-parent banks and
  target-specific local-load estimates.
- Continue balanced-factorization and finite side-length searches.
