# Open bottlenecks and research roadmap

## 1. Why inherited context is still necessary

Exact normalized four-endpoint traps exist. A proof based only on the current
small-board type and triple potential cannot establish global descent. Both the
prime-field and prime-power routes require inherited parent context, protected
reserve, ancestry, or envelope expansion.

The live branch now carries those labels through line-clean selectors,
protected matchings, sparse product skeletons, essential contractions, and
strict child-prefix recursion. No line, token, edge, skeleton, or factor charge
is anonymous.

2. **CMR219--CMR359:** primitive-height localization, thin-blocker signatures,

5. **CMR492--CMR557:** universal compatible-pair line-clean cylinders,
   minimum-restoration factorization, weighted cheap-clean selection, persistent

6. **CMR558--CMR604:** static collateral line decomposition, carry conversion,
   protected absorption, disjoint-conflict deletion, recurrent unavailable-set
   batching, near-static slack, persistent-core amplification, and complete

   protected-core sparse-interface factorization, skeleton recurrence, cross-edge

The branch is no longer missing cheap line-clean selection, selector ownership,

## 3. Current principal bottleneck: recurrent child-product potential

Fix a pure residual factor host `H` of side `d` inside a prime-power parent of
side `t=p^h`. CMR656--CMR663 attach to it:

- one deepest full-prefix envelope;
- one first nontrivial child split;
- an exact `p`-by-`p` transport table for every factor matching;
- at most `p^{2d}` vertex-routing skeletons;
- and, under one fixed routing skeleton, the exact product
  \[
  \prod_{r,s}
  \operatorname{PM}(H[X_{rs},Y_{rs}])
  \]
  over strict child prefix cells.

The unresolved theorem is now inside one recurrent routing skeleton.

### Required child-product theorem

For one fixed owner-labelled child product, prove at least one of:

1. one combination of child matchings is candidate-conflict-free;
2. one child factor carries a strictly smaller pure obstruction and recursion
   continues;
3. every dirty combination is covered by a sparse cross-child mixed atom,
   yielding another rank-at-most-two rectangle and deletion/contraction payment;
4. the routing skeleton changes often enough to pay full-token churn or
   closure-envelope expansion;
5. a heavy child block or dispersed child-token family pays reserve depletion,
   deletion ancestry, or full-token return.

The important new constraint is that child routing, absolute prefix coordinates,
selector ownership, and all contracted/deleted edges must remain fixed in the
charge.

## 4. Secondary bottlenecks

### Cross-child mixed-atom stock

CMR630 bounds mixed atoms for a two-block protected/free product. The same
rectangle mechanism should be written for the at most `p^2` strict child factors
of CMR659. A useful theorem would bound every cross-child conflict by a small
carrier set and extract either one low-rank prescription in a large child factor
or a bounded collection of small-child triggers.

### Routing-change ancestry

CMR661 gives finite routing-skeleton stock but does not yet assign a physical
edge price to every routing change. The desired ledger should express a change
of vertex-routing skeleton as entering/leaving edges across child prefix cells,
then apply the exact token incidence of CMR413 and the recreation support of
CMR418--CMR421.

### Fixed essential-core conflicts

CMR653 may terminate with a collinear triple entirely inside the accumulated
essential core. CMR643--CMR648 price escape from a fixed product certificate,
but the recursive ancestry of the same forced triple across child-envelope
changes still needs a compact owner-labelled ledger.

### Prime-field transfer

The complementary-hyperbola route still needs the inherited-envelope,
minimum-cost, selector-ownership, and strict child-product endpoint now available
on the prime-power route.

### Arbitrary side lengths

Even a complete prime-power theorem would not cover every `n`. Remaining options
include additional balanced prime factorizations, controlled products, CRT
absorption, or patching between nearby admissible sizes.

## 5. Open lemmas in recommended order

1. **Multi-child conflict rectangle.** Generalize CMR629--CMR635 from two factors
   to one fixed CMR659 child-routing product and isolate a low-rank prescription
   in one child or a bounded small-child trigger.
2. **Routing-change edge support.** Prove that every change of routing skeleton
   has a nonempty entering/leaving edge set crossing child prefix cells and
   attach exact full-token incidence.
3. **Child-recursion potential.** Show that repeated pure obstruction in one
   strict child factor decreases a lexicographic measure consisting of envelope
   depth, factor side, and host-edge count.
4. **Forced-core ancestry.** Deduplicate fixed essential-core triples across
   routing and envelope owners and charge their disappearance to CMR648 witness
   edges.
5. **Low-height and prime-field transfer.** Integrate the remaining thin
   signatures and rebuild the endpoint for prime fields.
6. **Balanced-family and CRT assembly.** Cover arbitrary side lengths.

## 6. Computational priorities

- Enumerate fixed child-routing products for small `p,h,d` and classify all
  cross-child conflict atoms by their child-rank vector.
- Measure the largest rectangle in completely dirty products of individually
  clean child factors.
- Verify routing-change entering/leaving support under arbitrary canonical
  forbidden matchings in original geometric coordinates.
- Test whether repeated forced essential-core triples stabilize under child
  recursion or force envelope expansion.
- Continue balanced-factorization and finite side-length searches.

## 7. Current proved endpoint

Through CMR663 the following are closed at their stated scales:

- exact harmonic-packet completion, reset accounting, and token recurrence;
- essential-core ancestry, exchange-corridor compression, and sparse rollback;
- minimum-cost tight-face, SCC, level, and colour normalization;
- mixed-cycle, theta-fan, rooted-star, and compatible-pair cylinder geometry;
- canonical line-clean selector ownership and dynamic unavailable-core payment;
- direct protected execution of heavy lines and secant stars;
- exact sparse-interface products and candidate-conflict rectangles;
- essential prescription transfer and forced-certificate escape payment;
- complete essential-core contraction and finite pure-factor recursion;
- canonical prime-power factor envelopes and strict child-routing products.

There is still no complete proof of the no-three-in-line conjecture.
