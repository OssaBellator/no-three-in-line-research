# The integer edge lattice of the strong-complete host

PX141 produces pseudorandom almost-perfect matchings in the exact-cover host
\(\mathcal H_p\). Exact absorption must respect the integer lattice generated by
its edges. This chapter computes that lattice completely.

Let \(A_p\) be the \(4p\times p^2\) incidence matrix whose column indexed by
\((x,y)\) is

\[
R_x+C_y+D_{x-y}+S_{x+y}.
\]

All labels are in \(\mathbb F_p\), while the incidence lattice itself is over
\(\mathbb Z\).

## Theorem PX142 -- PROVED

For every odd prime \(p\), the Smith normal form of \(A_p\) has

\[
4p-6
\]

unit invariant factors, followed by three invariant factors equal to \(p\), and
three zero rows. Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{coker}A_p
\cong
\mathbb Z^3\oplus(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^3.
}
\]

### Proof

Every edge has one vertex in each part, so the three differences among the four
part totals lie in the left kernel. Hence

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}A_p\le4p-3.
\]

Delete the last row from each of the \(C,D,S\) parts. Select all columns
\(e(x,y)\) with \(x\in\{0,1,2\}\), together with the columns \(e(x,0)\) for
\(3\le x<p\). This gives a square \((4p-3)\)-minor.

Expanding along the rows \(R_x\) for \(x\ge3\) reduces the determinant to the
three-row-layer cyclic matrix on \(x=0,1,2\). Fourier diagonalisation in the
\(y\)-coordinate gives, for every nontrivial \(p\)-th root \(q\), the block

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1\\
1&q&q^2\\
1&q^{-1}&q^{-2}
\end{pmatrix},
\]

whose determinant is

\[
\frac{(q-1)^3(q+1)}{q^2}.
\]

The zero Fourier mode contributes a unit. Using

\[
\prod_{q^p=1,\ q\ne1}(q-1)=\pm p,
\qquad
\prod_{q^p=1,\ q\ne1}(q+1)=1,
\qquad
\prod_{q^p=1,\ q\ne1}q=1,
\]

shows that the selected minor has determinant \(\pm p^3\). Therefore the
rational rank is \(4p-3\), no prime other than \(p\) divides a nonzero Smith
factor, and the product of all nonzero Smith factors divides \(p^3\).

It remains to compute the rank modulo \(p\). A left-kernel vector consists of
four functions \(a,b,c,d:\mathbb F_p\to\mathbb F_p\) satisfying

\[
a(x)+b(y)+c(x-y)+d(x+y)=0
\]

for all \(x,y\). Taking mixed finite differences shows that the third
differences of \(c\) and \(d\) vanish. Thus they are quadratic polynomials.
The mixed \(xy\)-coefficient forces their quadratic coefficients to agree, and
then \(a,b\) are determined up to the three constant part-total relations.
The solution space has dimension six: three constant relations, two linear
relations, and one quadratic relation. Hence

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_p}A_p=4p-6.
\]

Exactly three nonzero Smith factors are therefore divisible by \(p\). Their
product is at least \(p^3\) and divides the displayed minor \(p^3\), so they are
all exactly \(p\). \(\square\)

## Theorem PX143 -- PROVED

Let

\[
v=(v_R,v_C,v_D,v_S)\in\mathbb Z^{4p}.
\]

Then \(v\) lies in the integer span of the host edges if and only if all four
part totals are equal and the following three congruences hold modulo \(p\):

\[
\boxed{
\sum_z z v_D(z)+\sum_z z v_S(z)-2\sum_z z v_R(z)=0,
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\sum_z z v_S(z)-\sum_z z v_D(z)-2\sum_z z v_C(z)=0,
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\sum_z z^2v_D(z)+\sum_z z^2v_S(z)
-2\sum_z z^2v_R(z)-2\sum_z z^2v_C(z)=0.
}
\]

### Proof

Every edge satisfies the three congruences because

\[
(x-y)+(x+y)=2x,
\qquad
(x+y)-(x-y)=2y,
\]

and

\[
(x-y)^2+(x+y)^2=2x^2+2y^2.
\]

The three total differences give the free \(\mathbb Z^3\) quotient from PX142.
The three displayed moment maps are independent: on balanced four-vertex
vectors supported at \(R_0,C_0,D_z,S_w\), the choices

\[
(z,w)=(1,0),(0,1),(0,2)
\]

produce the matrix

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1&1\\
1&1&1\\
2&2&4
\end{pmatrix}
\]

with determinant \(4\ne0\pmod p\). Thus they realise the complete
\((\mathbb Z/p)^3\) quotient from PX142, and there are no further lattice
conditions. \(\square\)

## Corollary PX143a -- PROVED

A leftover containing one vertex from each part is lattice-admissible if and
only if those four vertices form a host edge. Thus every lattice-admissible
one-edge leftover completes automatically.

## 2. Two-point leftovers

Let each leftover part have size two. Write the sets uniquely as

\[
R_0=\{r-a,r+a\},
\qquad
C_0=\{c-b,c+b\},
\]

with \(a,b\ne0\). The first two moment conditions force

\[
D_0=\{r-c-u,r-c+u\},
\qquad
S_0=\{r+c-v,r+c+v\}
\]

for nonzero \(u,v\).

## Theorem PX144 -- PROVED

The two-point leftover is lattice-admissible exactly when

\[
\boxed{
u^2+v^2=2(a^2+b^2).}
\]

It is directly completable by two host edges exactly when

\[
\boxed{
\{u^2,v^2\}
=
\{(a-b)^2,(a+b)^2\}.
}
\]

### Proof

The linear conditions determine the centres of \(D_0,S_0\). Substitution into
the quadratic condition cancels the centre terms and gives the norm identity.

There are only two bijections between the two row vertices and the two column
vertices. Pairing equal signs produces difference and sum half-gaps
\(a-b,a+b\); pairing opposite signs interchanges them. Since a two-point set
\(\{z,-z\}\) depends only on \(z^2\), the displayed unordered-square criterion
is necessary and sufficient. \(\square\)

Thus a lattice-valid noncompletable two-point leftover is exactly a nontrivial
rotation on the circle

\[
u^2+v^2=2(a^2+b^2)
\]

which is not one of the signed coordinate permutations of
\((a-b,a+b)\). This is the first genuine absorber target.

## 3. Consequence for completion

PX141 leaves balanced vertex sets. PX143 shows that any leftover produced by a
matching automatically satisfies exactly three modular moment conditions and no
others. Exact completion therefore reduces to absorbing bounded balanced sets
subject to these moments.

The previous two-linear-invariant target was incomplete: the quadratic moment
explains the failed absorber searches for examples such as

\[
R_0=C_0=\{0,1\},
\quad
D_0=\{1,-1\},
\quad
S_0=\{0,2\},
\]

whose two linear moments vanish but whose quadratic defect is nonzero.
