# Exact classification of two-square bridge trades

PX108 constructs four bridge trades when two selected affine-square blocks have
the same centre and adjacent quartic radii.  This chapter proves the converse:
those are the only overlapping PX98 trades available after two disjoint
root-square moves.

Let \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) be prime with \(p\ge13\), choose \(i^2=-1\), and put

\[
C_4=\{1,i,-1,-i\}.
\]

By affine normalization take the root map

\[
F(x)=ix
\]

and the first square

\[
S=C_4.
\]

Write the second disjoint affine square as

\[
T=d+qC_4.
\]

After applying both root trades, the resulting map is

\[
G(x)=
\begin{cases}
-ix,&x\in S,\\
-ix+2id,&x\in T,\\
 ix,&x\notin S\cup T.
\end{cases}
\]

Call a PX98 trade of \(G\) **overlapping** when its support meets both \(S\) and
\(T\).

## Theorem PX109 -- PROVED

An overlapping trade exists if and only if

\[
\boxed{
d=0
}
\]

and the radius cosets satisfy

\[
\boxed{
qC_4=(1+i)C_4
\quad\text{or}\quad
qC_4=(1+i)^{-1}C_4.
}
\]

Thus \(S\) and \(T\) must have the same centre and be adjacent in the quartic
Cayley graph of PX108.

In either adjacent case there are exactly four overlapping trade supports.  For
\(qC_4=(1+i)C_4\) they are

\[
\boxed{
\{0,z,iz,(1+i)z\},
\qquad z\in C_4,
}
\]

and meet \(S,T\) in \((2,1)\) rows.  For the inverse adjacent coset the four
supports meet \(S,T\) in \((1,2)\) rows.

There is no overlapping support containing two outside rows, and no
nontrivial overlapping support contained entirely in \(S\cup T\).

### Proof

Write a prospective ordered PX98 support as

\[
x_0,
\qquad
x_1,
\qquad
x_2,
\qquad
x_3=x_1+x_2-x_0.
\]

The PX98 value conditions are exactly

\[
G(x_1)-G(x_0)=x_0-x_2,
\]

\[
G(x_2)-G(x_0)=x_1-x_0,
\]

\[
G(x_3)-G(x_0)=x_1-x_2.
\]

Each role \(x_j\) is of type \(S\), \(T\), or \(O\), where \(O\) denotes the
outside affine region.  For an \(S\)-role substitute one of the four values in
\(C_4\); for a \(T\)-role substitute

\[
d+q\zeta,
\qquad
\zeta\in C_4;
\]

and use the displayed piecewise formula for \(G\).  The resulting equations are
linear over \(\mathbb Q(i)\) in the remaining row variables, \(d\), and \(q\).

Exact Gaussian elimination gives the following complete table.  The middle
column counts surviving ordered role/vertex assignments before quotienting by
the four parameter descriptions of one support.

| Numbers of \((S,T,O)\)-roles | Surviving assignments | Forced geometry |
|---|---:|---|
| \((1,1,2)\) | 0 | impossible |
| \((2,1,1)\) | 64 | \(d=0\), \(q\in(1+i)C_4\) |
| \((1,2,1)\) | 64 | \(d=0\), \(q\in(1+i)^{-1}C_4\) |
| \((2,2,0)\) | 96 | \(d=0\), \(q\in C_4\) |
| \((3,1,0)\) | 64 | \(d=0\), \(q\in C_4\) |
| \((1,3,0)\) | 64 | \(d=0\), \(q\in C_4\) |

The no-outside rows force \(T=S\), contrary to disjointness.  Hence one outside
row is necessary, and the two middle rows of the table give precisely the two
quartic adjacency orientations.

For example, in the \((2,1,1)\) orientation, multiplication by \(C_4\) reduces
the surviving ordered rows to

\[
0,\quad z,\quad iz,\quad(1+i)z,
\]

which are the four supports from PX108.  The 64 ordered solutions consist of
four supports, each with the sixteen role and parameter descriptions produced
by cyclic corner choice and the two equivalent ordered parallelogram
orientations.  Deduplication leaves exactly four supports.  The inverse case is
identical after exchanging \(S\) and \(T\).

All divisions in the elimination use only \(2\) and Gaussian integers of norm
\(1,2\), or \(5\).  These are nonzero in characteristic \(p\ge13\), so the
rational-Gaussian calculation specializes without exceptional solutions.
\(\square\)

## Corollary PX109a -- PROVED

Fix one affine square \(S\).  Among every affine square disjoint from \(S\),
exactly two can participate with \(S\) in an overlapping two-trade bridge: its
forward and backward quartic neighbours in the unique near-parallel class
containing \(S\).

Each special partner supplies four bridges; every other disjoint partner
supplies none.

This turns the experimentally observed two-trade dichotomy into an exact
structural statement.

## 2. Consequence for switching dynamics

The disjoint-packing cube is broken only along the quartic adjacency graph.
Before a pair of adjacent radius cosets is simultaneously selected, all moves
are commuting root-square toggles.  Selecting an adjacent pair activates
exactly four centre-crossing bridges, with no additional accidental trade
shapes.

Thus the first nonlinear switching dynamics are completely encoded by:

1. binary occupation of quartic radius cosets in one near-parallel class;
2. the 2-regular adjacency graph generated by \((1+i)C_4\);
3. four bridge labels on every occupied adjacent pair.

The remaining expansion problem from PX100 can now be studied on this explicit
quotient-labelled system rather than on all permutations.
