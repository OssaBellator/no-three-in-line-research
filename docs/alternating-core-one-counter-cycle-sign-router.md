# Cycle-sign potentials for finite-control one-counter memory

**Branch:** `research/alternating-core-chain`

AC3so--AC3ss close every fixed regular-language history field when numerical memory is bounded. This note advances the remaining unbounded additive case. A finite control graph with integer counter increments either has a sign-coherent cycle family and therefore one global shortest-path potential, or it exposes explicit positive and negative simple-cycle addresses.

## One-counter model

Fix one epoch. Let `Y` be a finite directed control-state set of size `q>=1`. Each legal transition `e:u->v` has an integer counter increment `b_e` with `|b_e|<=W`. The live counter update is

`c'=c+b_e`.

All zero tests, guards, payment balances and terminal fields affecting future legality are assumed to be included in the decorated control state. Changing the graph or increment table is an outer reset.

## AC3st -- finite simple-cycle drift dictionary -- PROVED

Every finite walk decomposes into a simple residual path and a multiset of directed simple cycles. Each simple cycle has length at most `q`, and the total counter displacement is the residual-path displacement plus the sum of the extracted cycle drifts.

### Proof

Whenever a walk repeats a control vertex, delete the segment between the first two occurrences and record that closed segment. Repeating this operation leaves a simple path. Apply the same deletion inside every recorded closed walk until every recorded component is a simple directed cycle. Counter displacement is additive under concatenation and deletion, so the exact drift identity is preserved. A simple path or cycle visits at most `q` vertices. QED.

Thus arbitrary long additive histories are generated by a finite simple-cycle address dictionary plus a simple residual path.

## AC3su -- nonnegative-cycle shortest-path potential -- PROVED

Assume every directed cycle has nonnegative total increment. Add a super-source with a zero-weight edge to every control state, and let `d(v)` be the minimum walk weight from that source to `v`. Then

`-(q-1)W<=d(v)<=0`

and every transition `e:u->v` satisfies

`b_e+d(u)-d(v)>=0`.

Consequently

`H_+(c,v)=c-d(v)`

is nondecreasing along every legal transition.

### Proof

There is no negative directed cycle, so a minimum walk may be chosen simple after deleting nonnegative cycles. It uses at most `q-1` original edges, giving the displayed range; the direct super-source edge gives `d(v)<=0`. Shortest-path optimality gives `d(v)<=d(u)+b_e`, which rearranges to the reduced-weight inequality. The change of `H_+` across `e` is exactly `b_e+d(u)-d(v)`. QED.

The potential differs from the physical counter by at most `(q-1)W`.

## AC3sv -- nonpositive-cycle reverse potential -- PROVED

Assume every directed cycle has nonpositive total increment. Apply AC3su to the negated increments and let `d_-(v)` be the resulting shortest-path labels. Then

`H_-(c,v)=c+d_-(v)`

is nonincreasing along every legal transition, with `-(q-1)W<=d_-(v)<=0`.

### Proof

For the negated edge weights, AC3su gives

`-b_e+d_-(u)-d_-(v)>=0`.

Rearranging shows that the change `b_e+d_-(v)-d_-(u)` of `H_-` is nonpositive. QED.

## AC3sw -- strict one-sign bounded-epoch bound -- PROVED

Assume every directed cycle has strictly positive total increment. In the reduced graph of AC3su, every edge has nonnegative integer reduced weight and the zero-reduced-weight edges form an acyclic digraph. Hence every block of `q` transitions increases `H_+` by at least one.

If the physical counter is restricted to an interval of width `R`, then `H_+` lies in an interval of width at most

`S=R+(q-1)W`,

and the epoch has fewer than

`q*(S+1)`

internal transitions. The symmetric statement holds when every directed cycle is strictly negative.

### Proof

A directed cycle of zero reduced weight would have zero original drift because the vertex labels telescope, contradicting strict positivity. Thus a path of zero-reduced edges has at most `q-1` edges. Every `q` transitions therefore contain a positive integer reduced increment. A nondecreasing integer quantity in an interval of width `S` has at most `S` strict increases, with fewer than `q` zero steps before the first and between successive increases. The displayed safe bound follows. QED.

## AC3sx -- one-counter cycle-sign router -- PROVED UNDER THE SIMPLE-CYCLE CONTRACT

One fixed finite-control additive counter epoch has one of four exact outcomes:

1. all simple-cycle drifts are nonnegative, giving the common rank `H_+`;
2. all simple-cycle drifts are nonpositive, giving the common reverse rank `H_-`;
3. there is a zero-drift simple cycle, which is a finite exact quotient/ticket address;
4. there are explicit positive- and negative-drift simple cycles, each of length at most `q`.

If the sign-coherent family is strict and the counter is physically bounded, AC3sw gives the explicit transition bound. If the counter is unbounded, AC3su or AC3sv still supplies a global monotone rank.

### Proof

Inspect the finite simple-cycle drift dictionary from AC3st. If both signs occur, choose one cycle of each sign. Otherwise all drifts have one weak sign and AC3su or AC3sv applies. Zero cycles are retained as finite exact recurrence addresses. Strictness plus boundedness invokes AC3sw. QED.

## Corrected AC4 memory frontier

Finite-control additive one-counter memory is no longer opaque when its simple cycles have one drift sign. The remaining numerical-memory frontier is mixed-sign simple-cycle interaction without tickets or a secondary rank, dynamic or unbounded control graphs, nonadditive updates, omitted guards or balances, and changes of law not declared as outer resets.
