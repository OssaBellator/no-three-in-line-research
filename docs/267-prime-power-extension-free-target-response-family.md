# The union of all target banks is one extension-free matching family

The fixed-target construction chooses a forbidden perfect matching `F` through the
target cell `e`, then chooses a response matching avoiding both `F` and the fixed
opposite layer `O`.  When `F` varies, the complete response union has a simpler
exact description: it is the family of all perfect matchings which avoid `O` and
omit `e`.

The reverse inclusion uses regular bipartite factorisation.  Given a proposed
response `R`, the complement of the two disjoint perfect matchings `O,R` is
`(n-2)`-regular.  If `R` omits `e`, that complement contains `e`, and a 1-factor of
the complement through `e` supplies a compatible forbidden extension.

Fix `n>=3`, a perfect matching `O` of `K_{n,n}`, and an edge `e notin O`.

## 1. Every edge of a regular bipartite graph lies in a 1-factor

### Theorem CMR1302 -- PROVED

Let `H` be a finite `d`-regular bipartite graph with `d>=1`.  Every edge of `H`
belongs to a perfect matching of `H`.

### Proof

Hall's theorem gives one perfect matching.  Removing it leaves a `(d-1)`-regular
bipartite graph; iterating decomposes all edges of `H` into `d` disjoint perfect
matchings.  Every edge belongs to exactly one member of that decomposition. ∎

This is the edge-containing form used implicitly in CMR1238.

## 2. Exact union identity

Let

\[
\mathcal E(e;O)
=
\{F:F\text{ is a perfect matching},\ e\in F,\ F\cap O=\varnothing\}.
\]

Define the union of degree-two response banks

\[
\mathcal U(O,e)
=
\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal E(e;O)}
\operatorname{PM}(K_{n,n}\setminus(O\cup F)).
\]

### Theorem CMR1303 -- PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal U(O,e)
=
\operatorname{PM}(K_{n,n}\setminus(O\cup\{e\})).
}
\]

### Proof

Every matching in the left side avoids `O` and the selected extension `F`, which
contains `e`.  Hence it belongs to the right side.

Conversely, let `R` be a perfect matching avoiding `O` and `e`.  The graph

\[
H=K_{n,n}\setminus(O\cup R)
\]

is `(n-2)`-regular and contains `e`.  By CMR1302, `e` belongs to a perfect matching
`F` of `H`.  Then `F` is disjoint from `O` and `R`, contains `e`, and

\[
R\in\operatorname{PM}(K_{n,n}\setminus(O\cup F)).
\]

Thus `R` belongs to the left side. ∎

The identity is physical and independent of prime-power coordinates.

## 3. Canonical extension of a proposed response

Fix total orders on perfect matchings.  For

\[
R\in\operatorname{PM}(K_{n,n}\setminus(O\cup\{e\})),
\]

let `F(O,e,R)` be the first perfect matching of
`K_{n,n}\setminus(O\cup R)` containing `e`.

### Theorem CMR1304 -- PROVED

The canonical extension is well defined and satisfies

\[
\boxed{
e\in F(O,e,R),
\quad
F(O,e,R)\cap O=\varnothing,
\quad
F(O,e,R)\cap R=\varnothing.}
\]

Consequently `R` is a valid response in the degree-two bank generated by its
canonical extension.

### Proof

The existence statement is the reverse-inclusion proof of CMR1303.  Fixed-order
tie breaking makes the choice unique. ∎

Thus one may search response states first and recover the forbidden extension
afterward.

## 4. Extension-free target optimization

For any physical potential `Phi`, define

\[
\boxed{
\mu_n(O,e)
=
\min_{R\in\operatorname{PM}(K_{n,n}\setminus(O\cup\{e\}))}
\Phi(O\cup R).
}
\]

### Theorem CMR1305 -- PROVED

The value `mu_n(O,e)` equals the minimum over every forbidden extension through
`e` and every response in its degree-two bank.

Every minimizing response can be executed using the canonical extension
`F(O,e,R)` of CMR1304.

### Proof

The two response families are equal by CMR1303, so their minima agree.  Apply
CMR1304 to a minimizing member. ∎

This removes one entire enumeration level from finite target searches.

## 5. Target destruction remains exact

### Theorem CMR1306 -- PROVED

Every response in `mathcal U(O,e)` omits the physical cell `e`, is disjoint from
`O`, and therefore destroys every old target containing `e` when united with the
fixed opposite layer.

### Proof

This is the defining right side of CMR1303. ∎

The extension-free union changes neither saturation nor target destruction.

## 6. Restricted-host lowering response

Let a current restricted host have selected minimum `S=O union M`, and suppose
`e in M` lies in an old target.  Let `R_*` attain `mu_n(O,e)` and put
`Q_*=O union R_*`.

### Theorem CMR1307 -- PROVED

If

\[
\Phi(Q_*)<\Phi(S),
\]

then exactly one of the following holds.

1. `R_*` is feasible in the current host and gives strict improvement.
2. Adding its missing edges creates a lowering expansion; canonical normalization
   accepts a lower minimum or contracts an added minimum-core edge.

The compatible forbidden extension may be recovered after selecting `R_*`.

### Proof

Use CMR1304 for the extension and the lowering-expansion argument CMR1290/CMR1299
for restricted availability. ∎

Thus finite ambient improvement searches need only optimize over response
matchings.

## 7. Complete union versus one bank

### Theorem CMR1308 -- PROVED

The extension-free family `mathcal U(O,e)` is generally larger than any one
degree-two bank.  It is appropriate for ambient existence and lowering-expansion
arguments.

For complete-bank blockage and the minimal unit-wall theorem, one must still fix a
single extension `F` and use the graph

\[
K_{n,n}\setminus(O\cup F).
\]

### Proof

CMR1303 is a union over extensions.  A missing-edge cover of the union need not be
an inclusion-minimal blocker of any one matching graph.  The Hall-wall argument
CMR1150--CMR1157 applies to a fixed graph's complete perfect-matching family. ∎

This scope distinction prevents an ambient optimization from being silently used
as a fixed-bank blocker theorem.

## 8. Extension-free endpoint

### Corollary CMR1309 -- PROVED

Fixed-target response analysis now has two exact complementary forms.

1. **Ambient optimization:** use
   \[
   \operatorname{PM}(K_{n,n}\setminus(O\cup\{e\}))
   \]
   and recover a forbidden extension after choosing a response.
2. **Pointwise bank control and blockage:** fix `F`, use the `(n-2)`-regular
   degree-two graph, its collateral envelope and its minimal unit-wall blocker.

The first form accelerates finite sides and searches for lower-potential states.
The second form supports universal expectation, local-envelope and Hall-wall
arguments.

No all-`n` theorem is claimed.  The response-union equality, canonical extension,
extension-free minima and scope separation are checked in
[`scripts/verify_prime_power_extension_free_response.py`](../scripts/verify_prime_power_extension_free_response.py).
