# Bridge trades between adjacent quartic blocks

PX105 proves that after one affine-root square trade, every further move is
either the reversal or a trade on a disjoint root square.  Therefore genuinely
new overlapping switchings first appear in the two-trade layer.

Let \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) be prime with \(p\ge13\), choose \(i^2=-1\), and put

\[
C_4=\{1,i,-1,-i\}.
\]

Fix an affine root

\[
F(x)=ix+b.
\]

For a centre \(c\), the blocks of the PX106 near-parallel class are

\[
B_q(c)=c+qC_4,
\qquad
qC_4\in\mathbb F_p^\ast/C_4.
\]

## 1. Quartic adjacency

Put

\[
\rho=(1+i)C_4
\]

in \(\mathbb F_p^\ast/C_4\).  Two blocks in one near-parallel class are
**adjacent** when their radius cosets differ by multiplication by \(\rho\) or
\(\rho^{-1}\).

The two neighbours are distinct for \(p\ge13\).  If
\(\rho=\rho^{-1}\), then \((1+i)^2=2i\in C_4\), so \(2^4=1\pmod p\), forcing
\(p\mid15\).  Hence quartic adjacency is a 2-regular Cayley graph on the
\((p-1)/4\) blocks of each near-parallel class.

## Theorem PX108 -- PROVED

Fix

\[
S=c+qC_4
\]

and its forward adjacent block

\[
T=c+q(1+i)C_4.
\]

Apply the affine-root PX98 trades on both \(S\) and \(T\).  For every
\(z\in C_4\), define

\[
U_z=c+qz\{0,1,i,1+i\}.
\]

Then:

1. the four sets \(U_z\) are distinct PX98 trade supports;
2. each contains the omitted centre \(c\);
3. each meets \(S\) in two rows and \(T\) in one row;
4. applying one leaves the disjoint-packing cube generated by \(S,T\).

For the backward adjacent block

\[
c+q(1+i)^{-1}C_4,
\]

the analogous four supports meet \(S\) in one row and the adjacent block in two
rows.

### Proof

Affine translation and scaling reduce to

\[
c=0,
\qquad
q=1,
\qquad
b=0.
\]

Thus

\[
S=C_4,
\qquad
T=(1+i)C_4,
\]

and after the two disjoint root trades,

\[
G(x)=
\begin{cases}
-ix,&x\in S\cup T,\\
 ix,&x\notin S\cup T.
\end{cases}
\]

Fix \(z\in C_4\) and use PX98 parameters

\[
a=0,
\qquad
r=z,
\qquad
s=-iz.
\]

The ordered rows are

\[
0,
\qquad
z,
\qquad
iz,
\qquad
(1+i)z,
\]

and their values are

\[
0,
\qquad
-iz,
\qquad
z,
\qquad
(1-i)z.
\]

These are exactly

\[
A,
\qquad
A+s,
\qquad
A+r,
\qquad
A+r+s
\]

with \(A=0\).  Hence the PX98 identities hold.  Multiplication by the four
elements of \(C_4\) gives four distinct supports.  The backward-neighbour case
follows after interchanging the adjacent radius cosets. \(\square\)

PX109 proves the converse: these are all two-square overlapping trades.

## 2. Exact bridge counts

### Corollary PX108a -- PROVED

Every affine-square block has two adjacent partners.  Each adjacent unordered
pair activates four bridge trades.

For a forward ordered pair \((S,T)\):

- every row of \(S\) belongs to two bridge supports;
- every row of \(T\) belongs to one bridge support;
- the centre belongs to all four.

For the backward partner, the roles reverse: every row of \(S\) belongs to one
bridge support and every row of the partner belongs to two.

Across the complete affine-square design there are

\[
\boxed{p\frac{p-1}{4}}
\]

adjacent unordered block pairs and

\[
\boxed{p(p-1)}
\]

labelled bridge opportunities.

### Proof

The quartic adjacency graph is 2-regular on \((p-1)/4\) vertices, so each
centre contributes \((p-1)/4\) unordered adjacent pairs.  Multiplying by the
\(p\) centres gives the first count, and PX108 gives four bridges per pair.

For the forward incidence, a row \(w\in S\) occurs when \(w=z\) or \(w=iz\),
while a row \(w\in T\) occurs only when \(w=(1+i)z\).  The backward incidence is
obtained by exchanging the two blocks. \(\square\)

## 3. Direct escape from one shell support

Let \(G_S\) be a one-trade shell mapping and fix a row \(x\in S\).

1. The forward adjacent partner supplies two bridge supports containing \(x\).
2. The backward adjacent partner supplies one bridge support containing \(x\).

Hence every original-support edge has exactly

\[
\boxed{3}
\]

explicit length-two bridge paths which remove it without first reversing to the
affine root.

This count is constant, not the \(\Omega(p)\) flow required by PX100.  Its role
is structural: the flow is no longer forced through the unique reverse edge,
and the endpoints lie outside all disjoint root-square packing cubes.

PX113--PX115 later amplify the shell flow to at least \(p-44\) paths under every
rank-at-most-two conditioning by using marker-preserving near-resolution
completion.

## 4. Deeper-layer target

The first nonlinear dynamics are encoded by the quotient

\[
\mathbb F_p^\ast/C_4:
\]

- isolated selected radius cosets support commuting root trades;
- adjacent selected cosets activate four centre-crossing bridges;
- bridge moves create a finite nonlinear core.

PX109 classifies the activation exactly, and PX112 classifies the complete
one-step shell after a bridge.  The remaining general problem is expansion from
bounded nonlinear cores with constant reverse congestion.
