# Bridge trades between adjacent quartic blocks

PX105 proves that after one affine-root square trade, every further move is
either the reversal or a trade on a disjoint root square.  Therefore genuinely
new overlapping switchings can first appear in the two-trade layer.

This chapter gives an explicit family of such second-order moves.  They occur
between adjacent blocks of the quartic near-resolution PX106 and pass through
the unique row omitted by that near-parallel class.

Let \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) be prime with \(p\ge13\), choose \(i^2=-1\), and put

\[
C_4=\{1,i,-1,-i\}.
\]

Fix an affine root

\[
F(x)=ix+b.
\]

For a centre \(c\), the blocks of the PX106 near-parallel class are

\[
B_q(c)=c+qC_4,
\qquad
qC_4\in\mathbb F_p^\ast/C_4.
\]

## 1. Quartic adjacency

Put

\[
\rho=(1+i)C_4
\]

in the quotient group \(\mathbb F_p^\ast/C_4\).  Call two blocks in one
near-parallel class adjacent when their radius cosets differ by multiplication
by \(\rho\) or \(\rho^{-1}\).

The two neighbours are distinct for \(p\ge13\).  Indeed, if
\(\rho=\rho^{-1}\), then

\[
(1+i)^2=2i\in C_4,
\]

so \(2\in C_4\).  This would imply \(2^4=1\pmod p\), hence \(p\mid15\), which is
impossible.

Thus quartic adjacency is a 2-regular Cayley graph on the \((p-1)/4\) blocks of
each near-parallel class.

## Theorem PX108 -- PROVED

Fix

\[
S=c+qC_4
\]

and its forward adjacent block

\[
T=c+q(1+i)C_4.
\]

Apply the affine-root PX98 trades on both \(S\) and \(T\), producing a strong
complete mapping \(G\).

For every \(z\in C_4\), define

\[
U_z
=
c+qz\{0,1,i,1+i\}.
\]

Then:

1. the four sets \(U_z\) are distinct PX98 trade supports available at \(G\);
2. each \(U_z\) contains the centre \(c\);
3. each \(U_z\) meets \(S\) in two rows and \(T\) in one row;
4. applying the trade on \(U_z\) leaves the disjoint-packing cube generated by
   \(S\) and \(T\).

For the backward adjacent block
\(c+q(1+i)^{-1}C_4\), the analogous four supports meet \(S\) in one row and the
adjacent block in two rows.

### Proof

Affine translation and scaling reduce to

\[
c=0,
\qquad
q=1,
\qquad
b=0.
\]

Thus

\[
S=C_4,
\qquad
T=(1+i)C_4.
\]

After the two disjoint root trades,

\[
G(x)=
\begin{cases}
-ix,&x\in S\cup T,\\
 ix,&x\notin S\cup T.
\end{cases}
\]

Fix \(z\in C_4\).  Use PX98 parameters

\[
a=0,
\qquad
r=z,
\qquad
s=-iz.
\]

The ordered support rows are

\[
0,
\qquad
z,
\qquad
iz,
\qquad
(1+i)z.
\]

The first row lies outside \(S\cup T\), the middle two lie in \(S\), and the
last lies in \(T\).  Their \(G\)-values are

\[
0,
\qquad
-iz,
\qquad
z,
\qquad
(1-i)z.
\]

These are exactly

\[
A,
\qquad
A+s,
\qquad
A+r,
\qquad
A+r+s
\]

with \(A=0\).  Hence the PX98 hypotheses hold and \(U_z\) is an available
trade support.

Multiplication by the four elements of \(C_4\) gives four distinct supports.
Each contains \(0\), two elements \(z,iz\) of \(S\), and the element
\((1+i)z\) of \(T\).  Undoing the affine normalization proves the first four
assertions.

The backward-neighbour statement follows by interchanging the two adjacent
radius cosets, equivalently by replacing \(i\) with \(-i\). \(\square\)

These bridge trades are genuinely second order.  PX105a rules them out after
only the trade on \(S\), because their support would meet \(S\) in two rows.
The modification on the adjacent block \(T\) supplies the third altered value
needed to make the PX98 equations hold.

## 2. Exact bridge counts

### Corollary PX108a -- PROVED

Every affine-square block has exactly two adjacent partners in its
near-parallel class.  Each adjacent unordered pair activates four bridge
trades.

For one fixed adjacent partner:

- every row of the first block belongs to exactly two of the four bridge
  supports;
- every row of the second block belongs to exactly one bridge support;
- the omitted centre belongs to all four bridge supports.

Across the complete affine-square design there are

\[
\boxed{
p\frac{p-1}{4}
}
\]

adjacent unordered block pairs and

\[
\boxed{
p(p-1)
}

labelled bridge opportunities.

### Proof

The quartic adjacency graph is 2-regular on \((p-1)/4\) vertices, so each
centre contributes \((p-1)/4\) unordered adjacent pairs.  Multiplying by the
\(p\) centres gives the first count.  PX108 supplies four bridges per pair.

For the incidence assertions, the four supports are indexed by \(z\in C_4\).
A row \(w\in S\) occurs when \(w=z\) or \(w=iz\), giving exactly two values of
\(z\).  A row \(w\in T\) occurs only when \(w=(1+i)z\), giving one value.  The
centre occurs for every \(z\). \(\square\)

## 3. Consequence for the original-support bottleneck

Let \(G_S\) be a one-trade shell mapping with original support \(S\).  PX105b
gives only one trade touching \(S\): the reversal.  PX108 gives a bounded
two-step escape.

1. Choose either adjacent block \(T\), which is disjoint from \(S\), and apply
   its root trade.
2. Choose a bridge support containing the desired row of \(S\).

For each row of \(S\), each adjacent partner supplies two such bridge supports.
Hence every original-support edge has four explicit length-two paths which
remove it without first reversing to the affine root.

The count is still constant rather than the \(\Omega(p)\) source flow required
by PX100.  Its significance is structural: the flow is no longer forced
through the unique reverse edge, and the resulting mappings are outside every
disjoint root-square packing family.

## 4. Revised deeper-layer target

The near-resolution and bridge system give a concrete local dynamics on the
quotient

\[
\mathbb F_p^\ast/C_4.
\]

- isolated selected radius cosets support only commuting root trades;
- adjacent selected cosets activate four centre-crossing bridges;
- bridge moves alter the adjacency pattern and can create further overlapping
  trades.

The next bottleneck is to show that repeated bridge activation expands from a
constant number of paths per original edge to \(\Omega(p)\) bounded-congestion
paths under every rank-at-most-two conditioning.  This is a finite-field
switching problem on quartic-coset sign patterns rather than an unrestricted
permutation search.
