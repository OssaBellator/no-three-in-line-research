# Recurrent entering target cells delete exactly inside one fixed owner

This chapter records CMR2982--CMR2993. CMR2968--CMR2981 reduce an unbounded
fixed-envelope target chain to one exact owner-labelled cell-target pair `(e,T)`
which is repeatedly recreated through the entering cell `e`. CMR713--CMR719
show that this is not a terminal obstruction: the preceding matching avoids
`e`, so deleting `e` preserves matchability and destroys the entire active target
star through `e`.

The executable checker is:

```text
scripts/check_prime_power_recurrent_target_edge_deletion_ancestry.py
```

## CMR2982 — literal fixed-owner factor context

A balanced one-layer factor host of side `m` is represented by its complete row
and column domains together with a canonical deleted-edge mask. Its feasible
family is generated by all perfect matchings avoiding that mask. The owner label
is the digest of the ambient side and exact context identity.

## CMR2983 — entering-edge nonessentiality

Let `M` and `N` be feasible parent matchings and let

\[
e\in N\setminus M.
\]

Then `M` is a literal avoidance certificate for `e`. Hence `e` is nonessential
and remains absent from at least one perfect matching after deletion.

This is the executable CMR713 implication.

## CMR2984 — exact recurrent-target deletion child

The typed transition


\[

The generated child family agrees exactly with

\[
\{S\in\mathcal F(D):e\notin S\}.
\]

The avoidance matching `M` is checked to survive in the child.

## CMR2985 — exact target-star destruction

For the complete active collinear-triple family of the parent host, define

\[
\mathcal A_e
=
\{Q:e\in Q\}.
\]

The checker enumerates this star exactly. Every member of `A_e`, including the
selected recurrent target `T`, is absent from the child active-triple family.

## CMR2986 — deletion cannot activate a target

Every child matching was already a parent matching. Therefore the complete
child active-triple set is a subset of the parent active-triple set. No target
or matching prescription becomes active merely because the host was restricted.

## CMR2987 — permanence until reintroduction or owner change

After deletion, further deletions and endpoint contractions cannot restore `e`.
Any later recurrence of a target containing the same physical edge therefore
requires either:

1. a genuine absent-to-present edge reintroduction; or
2. a transition to a different owner whose host contains that edge.

This is the exact CMR715 ownership boundary.
