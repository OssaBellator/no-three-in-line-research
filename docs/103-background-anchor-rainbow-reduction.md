# Two-replacement collateral is simultaneous rainbow failure

PX189--PX190 remove the internal rank-three logarithm from every
first-generation neutralization bank. The remaining difficult term is

\[
T_2,
\]

counting triples with two replacement cells and one fixed background point.
This chapter gives its exact combinatorial normal form.

For each fixed background point, the candidate rematching cells carry a proper
line-direction edge-colouring. Pair collateral is exactly the number of
repeated colours in the selected perfect matching. Thus eliminating \(T_2\) is
a simultaneous-rainbow perfect-matching problem.

Let \(R,C\subset\mathbb Z\) be distinct row and column sets of equal size, and
let

\[
G\subseteq R\times C
\]

be the allowed rematching graph after forbidden positions are removed. Let
\(Z\) be the fixed background point set, disjoint from every cell that may be
selected.

## 1. Anchor colourings

Fix \(z=(z_R,z_C)\in Z\). For a candidate cell \(e=(r,c)\), normally colour it
by the unoriented primitive direction of the line \(ze\):

\[
\chi_z(e)
=
\frac{(r-z_R,c-z_C)}{\gcd(|r-z_R|,|c-z_C|)}
\quad\text{up to sign}.
\]

There are two axis degeneracies.

- If \(r=z_R\), give \(e\) a private row-axis colour determined by \(c\).
- If \(c=z_C\), give \(e\) a private column-axis colour determined by \(r\).

This tie-breaking does not change any collision between two compatible matching
edges: two selected matching edges cannot share a row or column, so a horizontal
or vertical triple through \(z\) is impossible anyway.

## Theorem PX191 -- PROVED

For every anchor \(z\in Z\), the colouring

\[
\chi_z:E(G)\to\mathcal C_z
\]

is a proper edge-colouring of the bipartite graph \(G\).

Moreover, for two compatible candidate cells \(e,e'\),

\[
\boxed{
\chi_z(e)=\chi_z(e')
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
z,e,e'\text{ are collinear}.
}
\]

### Proof

Fix one source row \(r\ne z_R\). A nonvertical line through \(z\) meets that row
in at most one cell, so distinct cells in the row have distinct direction
colours. If \(r=z_R\), the private row-axis colours are distinct by definition.
The column argument is identical, proving properness.

For compatible cells, neither the horizontal nor vertical tie-breaking can
produce an equal colour. Equality of their remaining primitive direction
colours means exactly that both lie on the same unoriented line through \(z\).
\(\square\)

## 2. Exact collision identity

Let \(M\) be one perfect matching of \(G\). For an anchor \(z\), write

\[
n_z(\gamma)
=
|\{e\in M:\chi_z(e)=\gamma\}|.
\]

## Theorem PX192 -- PROVED

The two-replacement/one-background collateral of \(M\) is exactly

\[
\boxed{
T_2(M;Z)
=
\sum_{z\in Z}
\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal C_z}
\binom{n_z(\gamma)}2.
}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
T_2(M;Z)=0
}
\]

if and only if \(M\) is rainbow in every anchor colouring
\(\chi_z\), \(z\in Z\).

### Proof

For one anchor, PX191 gives a bijection between equal-colour pairs of matching
edges and triples consisting of that anchor and those two replacement cells.
Summing over colours counts each pair for that anchor once. Summing over anchors
counts exactly the geometric \(T_2\) certificates, including their correct
multiplicity when several background points lie on one candidate secant line.
\(\square\)

## 3. Relevant anchor family

An anchor contributes nothing unless some line through it contains at least two
candidate cells. Thus one may discard every \(z\in Z\) for which all colour
classes have size at most one.

Let

\[
Z_{R,C}
=
\{z\in Z:\exists e\ne e'\in R\times C,\ z,e,e'\text{ collinear}\}.
\]

Only the colourings indexed by \(Z_{R,C}\) matter. If every scalar line contains
at most \(L\) background points, then

\[
|Z_{R,C}|
\le
L\binom{|R|^2}{2},
\]

because every relevant anchor lies on a secant line of the candidate grid.
This is still polynomially large in the block size and therefore is not covered
by the fixed-family theorem PX173.

## 4. Relation to the protected-direction frontier

PX192 shows that the repair and protected-spread routes meet at the same exact
object.

- In direction protection, one asks for a perfect matching rainbow in a family
  of linear-form colourings.
- In second-generation repair, one asks for a perfect matching rainbow in the
  family of projective line pencils centred at the relevant background points.

The latter family can have polynomial size even after square-root thinning.
Thus the unresolved \(T_2\) term is not a loose counting estimate: it is a
growing-family simultaneous-rainbow matching problem.

There are nevertheless two advantages over the global PX175 problem.

1. The colourings are proper on an ordinary bipartite rematching graph of order
   only \(s=\Theta(\sqrt t)\).
2. The relevant anchors and all colour collisions are generated by the current
   selected background, so a failed rainbow selection yields a concrete
   geometric concentration that may support another executable batch.

## 5. Revised repair target

After PX190 and PX192, a complete second-generation theorem may take either of
two forms.

### Exact rainbow form

Show that every degree-two-forbidden rematching graph arising from a decoder
endpoint has a perfect matching simultaneously rainbow in all relevant anchor
colourings.

### Decoder-or-structure form

Show that either:

1. such a rainbow or low-collision matching exists; or
2. the anchor-colour conflict system contains a large star, biclique, or
   low-complexity pencil family that can be neutralized by a new batch.

The second form is more compatible with the existing transposition-decoder
philosophy. It does not require a black-box growing-family rainbow theorem for
arbitrary proper colourings.

A natural next statistic is the anchor collision degree

\[
\deg_z(e)
=
|\{e'\in E(G): e'\text{ compatible with }e,
\chi_z(e')=\chi_z(e)\}|.
\]

Large aggregate \(T_2\) with bounded selected-line occupancy must force many
candidate edges with large summed anchor collision degree. The next useful
lemma should convert that weighted degree into either a removable set of
positions or a common geometric pencil bank.
