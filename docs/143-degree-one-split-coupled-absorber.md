# Coupled absorption of the degree-one transitive split core

The ordinary first-generation product bank avoids the current diagonal and one
opposite permutation layer.  In the decomposition

\[
F=D\cup F_0\cup H_1\cup\cdots\cup H_d,
\]

the nonhistorical base graph `F_0` is therefore one partial matching and has

\[
\Delta_0=1.
\]

PX331 reduces every no-composite-reset trajectory antichain to order at most
two.  The unique nontrivial equality case has one base orientation and the
reverse historical orientation.  Because the base edge is generated by an
endpoint in the opposite permutation layer, a coupled move can remove it.

## 1. The unique degree-one split core

### Theorem PX334 -- PROVED

In the ordinary two-layer base model `Delta_0=1`, every trajectory antichain
child either has a composite historical-cycle reset or has order at most two.

If the residual order is two, then after relabelling its off-diagonal cells are

\[
\boxed{
u\to v\in H_j,
\qquad
v\to u\in F_0
}
\]

for one historical level `H_j`.  This is the unique nontrivial transitive split
core.

### Proof

PX331 gives `s<=Delta_0+1=2`.  At `s=2`, both off-diagonal cells are forbidden.
Acyclicity of the historical union permits at most one of them to be
historical.  Absence of an allowed cell forces the other to be base.  The
edge-minimal form has exactly the displayed split. \(\square\)

## 2. Moving the base-edge generator

Assume the base partial matching `F_0` is induced by an auxiliary selected
matching block `B`.  The base edge `v->u` is generated by one current auxiliary
endpoint `q`.  Transposing `q` with a partner `q'` removes `v->u` from `F_0` and
inserts one new base edge `b(q')`.

Assume distinct partner choices produce distinct inserted base edges, as they
do for a permutation-layer partial matching.

Let the auxiliary block have order `n` and inherited forbidden degree
`Delta_2`.

### Theorem PX335 -- PROVED

The generator endpoint `q` has at least

\[
\boxed{n-1-2\Delta_2}
\]

admissible auxiliary transposition partners.

Among those partners, at most one inserts the historical primary edge
`u->v` into the new base partial matching.

### Proof

The partner count is PX323 applied to the auxiliary block.  Injectivity of the
permutation-layer generator map implies that at most one partner choice can
produce any prescribed new base edge, in particular `u->v`. \(\square\)

## 3. Coupled split-core reset

### Theorem PX336 -- PROVED

If

\[
\boxed{n\ge2\Delta_2+3,}
\]

there is an admissible auxiliary transposition which removes the base edge
`v->u` and does not insert `u->v`.

Release the historical level containing `u->v` and execute this auxiliary
transposition.  Then both primary cross cells are allowed, and the principal
primary transposition

\[
\boxed{u\leftrightarrow v}
\]

is executable.

### Proof

PX335 gives at least two admissible auxiliary partners.  At most one is the
collision partner which would insert `u->v`, so choose another.  The auxiliary
move deletes the unique base obstruction `v->u`; releasing `H_j` deletes the
unique historical obstruction `u->v`.  The chosen auxiliary replacement does
not recreate either obstruction.  The two primary off-diagonal cells therefore
form an allowed transposition. \(\square\)

The primary and auxiliary moves may be evaluated by the dependent two-block
ledger PX253--PX255.

## 4. Final degree-one trajectory interface

### Corollary PX337 -- PROVED

For the ordinary degree-one product base, every trajectory-saturated terminal
core has one of the following outcomes.

1. a historical-cycle reset;
2. a coupled primary/auxiliary reset by PX336;
3. an auxiliary residual block of order

   \[
   \boxed{n\le2\Delta_2+2,}
   \]

   which belongs to the exact dependent two-block terminal optimizer PX274.

Thus no trajectory obstruction of growing order remains in the degree-one
base model.  The unresolved object is a fixed-size dependent two-block
terminal certificate.

### Proof

Apply PX334.  In the two-label case use PX336 unless its size hypothesis fails.
Failure gives the displayed auxiliary terminal bound, and PX274 enumerates the
coupled state exactly. \(\square\)
