# Prime-seed relative-cycle and maximal-line diagnostic

Run:

```bash
python scripts/check_prime_seed_relative_cycles.py \
  experiments/prime-seed-relative-cycle-example.json
```

For the stored certificates the checker reports:

```text
p=3:  relative cycles [2],                       maximal lines checked 0
p=5:  relative cycles [2,2],                     maximal lines checked 14
p=7:  relative cycles [6],                       maximal lines checked 58
p=11: relative cycles [10],                      maximal lines checked 406
p=13: relative cycles [4,4,2,2],                 maximal lines checked 898
p=37: relative cycles [14,14,6,2],               maximal lines checked 70798
p=41: relative cycles [38,2],                    maximal lines checked 108270
p=43: relative cycles [34,6,2],                  maximal lines checked 131426
p=47: relative cycles [11,11,11,11,2],           maximal lines checked 188026
p=53: relative cycles [10,8,8,6,6,6,6,2],       maximal lines checked 309174
p=59: relative cycles [14,14,14,14,2],           maximal lines checked 476358
```

Every relative permutation is fixed-point-free, every `tau` layer is exactly
reconstructed as `sigma o pi`, and every maximal grid line contains at most two
selected cells.

The maximal-line generator uses primitive direction vectors and starts each
line at the unique grid point whose predecessor lies outside the board. It
therefore generates every nonaxis maximal line once, together with all `2n`
row and column lines.

The `p=37` certificate was generated by the exact support-thirteen swapped
quarter-turn repair recorded in `docs/292`. The `p=41,43,53` certificates are
the independently decoded public archive records recorded in `docs/301`. The
`p=47` certificate is the independently decoded public RLE record recorded in
`docs/294`. The `p=59` certificate is independently reconstructed from the
attributed Wikimedia coordinate record in `docs/295`.

The checker validates exact finite certificates and the compressed constraint
bookkeeping. It does not establish asymptotic feasibility for prime-minus-one
boards.
